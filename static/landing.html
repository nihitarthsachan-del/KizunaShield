A new law is changing what SMEs owe regulators.

Japan's Cyber Response Capability Enhancement Act pushes reporting obligations further down the size ladder than before, into organizations that have never had to formalize incident response.

Most of the small and mid-sized companies affected have no dedicated security team, no asset inventory, and no existing process for turning a scan into a document a regulator will accept. KizunaShield is built to close that specific gap — cheaply, and before the date above.

Four steps from unknown exposure to a filed report

  1. 1

    Discover

    Pull in exposed assets — RDP, FTP, SMB shares, IoT admin panels, OT/SCADA endpoints, open databases — from a scan or from data you already have.

  2. 2

    Score

    Each asset gets a base risk score by exposure type, boosted when it matches a known-exploited vulnerability, boosted again if that CVE has documented ransomware use.

  3. 3

    Report

    Generate a compliance-ready PDF: executive summary, asset inventory, matched vulnerabilities, and a readiness note — no manual formatting.

  4. 4

    Remediate

    Work down a prioritized fix list ordered by what actually reduces ransomware risk first, not just by severity label.
